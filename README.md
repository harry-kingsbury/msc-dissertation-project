Accurate data-driven modeling of non-linear physiological time series, such as cardiac electrocardiogram (ECG) and neural electroencephalogram (EEG) signals, is critical for mechanistic understanding and biomedical applications. However, biological signals are inherently noisy, non-stationary, and multiscale, posing major challenges for system identification. Standard classical techniques like Dynamic Mode Decomposition and Sparse Identification of Nonlinear Dynamics struggle to balance predictive accuracy with model interpretability, often sacrificing one for the other. Quantum machine learning offers access to high-dimensional feature spaces capable of representing complex non-linear mappings, motivating this research to investigate whether and how quantum-enhanced architectures can improve the accuracy-interpretability trade-off in physiological modeling.

To address this research question, a unified computational pipeline was engineered to benchmark classical baselines against quantum-enhanced methods across six diverse clinical datasets, comprising three PhysioNet ECG cohorts and three OpenNeuro EEG task and resting-state datasets. The experimental framework implemented Dynamic Mode Decomposition, standard Sparse Identification of Nonlinear Dynamics, and gradient-optimized AdamSINDy alongside two quantum approaches: a standalone Quantum Feature Map model and a hybrid Quantum Sparse Identification architecture. All signals underwent standardized pre-processing and trial-aware train-test splits before being evaluated through multi-step forecast rollouts to test long-horizon stability and predictive accuracy across modalities.

Empirical evaluation revealed distinct performance patterns across modalities and model choices. The standalone Quant
um Feature Map achieved the lowest raw forecasting errors across both heart and brain data; however, its classical linear readout collapsed into a near-constant predictor that failed to recover explicit physical dynamics. Conversely, classical sparse regression and the polynomial-only hybrid variant successfully identified clean, transparent governing differential equations with competitive forecast accuracy. Directly inserting quantum-generated features into the sparse candidate equation library severely degraded performance due to coefficient cannibalisation, where quantum terms distorted the learned physical dynamics.

This work demonstrates that quantum-enhanced machine learning is not a universal remedy for physiological modeling, but its efficacy depends entirely on architectural integration. Quantum circuits provide substantial value when acting as non-linear feature extractors or preprocessing transformers for complex neural trajectories, whereas equation discovery performs best when preserving classical polynomial bases. By systematically mapping the trade-offs between predictive precision, numerical stability, and symbolic interpretability, this dissertation establishes a grounded framework for deploying hybrid quantum-classical algorithms in biomedical data science.
